Task 1 · Task_1_.ipynb

Data Preparation &
Customer Analytics

Examine and clean transaction and customer data, then build segment-level insights to guide strategic chip category recommendations.

Datasets

📊
QVI_transaction_data.xlsx
Transaction-level sales records including product name, quantity, total sales, date, and loyalty card number. Covers the full 2018–2019 fiscal year.
👥
QVI_purchase_behaviour.csv
Customer segmentation data mapping loyalty card numbers to lifestage categories (e.g., Older Families, Retirees) and premium tier (Budget / Mainstream / Premium).

Methodology

Step 01
Data Cleaning
Removed duplicates, handled nulls, detected outliers via IQR on TOT_SALES, and identified Dec 25 as a valid store-closed gap.
Step 02
Feature Engineering
Extracted pack sizes (grams) from product names, standardised brand names (e.g. Dorito → Doritos), and created a combined segment column.
Step 03
Segment Analysis
Aggregated total sales, unique customers, purchase frequency, and average spend per LIFESTAGE × PREMIUM_CUSTOMER group.
Step 04
Significance Testing
Ran Welch's t-test on Mainstream vs non-Mainstream Young/Midage Singles — confirmed statistically significant spending difference (p ≈ 0).

Key Findings

F01
The top 3 revenue segments are Older Families (Budget) at $156,864 · Young Singles/Couples (Mainstream) at $147,582 · and Retirees (Mainstream) at $145,169 — together accounting for 25% of total chip sales.
F02
Older Families rank #1 in sales not due to population size, but because of their exceptional purchase frequency — the highest of any segment.
F03
Older and Young Families buy the greatest average quantity of chips per visit, making them high-value targets for multi-pack or bundle promotions.
F04
The Mainstream Young & Midage Singles/Couples segment spends the most per purchase. The gap versus Budget/Premium counterparts is statistically significant (Welch's t-test, p ≈ 0).
F05
Kettle is the dominant brand across every segment. Doritos is uniquely preferred by Young/Midage Mainstream Singles as their second choice — differing from all other segments that favour Smiths.
F06
The 175g pack size is the top choice universally, followed by 150g. Stocking these sizes prominently across all channels is a straightforward revenue lever.
F07
Sales spike steadily toward Christmas, with a single zero-transaction day on Dec 25 (store closed). This seasonal ramp-up represents a strong opportunity for targeted pre-Christmas promotions.

Commercial Recommendations

Older Families · Budget
Frequency & Volume Promotions
Deploy loyalty-based offers that reward repeat visits and multi-pack purchases. This segment already visits frequently — nudging them to buy slightly more per trip has an outsized revenue impact.
Young Singles/Couples · Mainstream
Doritos Partnership Campaign
This is the only segment where Doritos beats Smiths as the second brand. A co-branded or shelf-placement campaign with Doritos specifically targeted at this group could capture spend away from competitors.
Retirees · Mainstream
Maximise Reach & Frequency
Their large population drives total sales volume. Prioritise broad promotional reach — catalogue features, weekly deals — to maintain high exposure across this sizable and loyal segment.
All Segments · General
Kettle + 175g as Hero SKU
Kettle is #1 across all segments and 175g is the universal top pack size. Feature this combination prominently in end-of-aisle displays and pre-Christmas promotional bundles to maximise category conversion.

Experimentation &
Uplift Testing

Evaluate the performance of a store format trial in stores 77, 86, and 88 by selecting statistically comparable control stores and measuring significant uplift.

Trial Stores
3
Stores 77, 86, and 88 evaluated across Feb–Apr 2019 using matched control pairs.
Significant Uplift
2/3
Stores 77 and 86 exceeded the 95% confidence threshold on both total sales and customer count in all trial months.
Composite Score
50/50
Control stores selected using equal weighting of Pearson correlation and normalised magnitude distance.

Control Store Selection Methodology

Each trial store was matched to a control store by computing a composite similarity score over the pre-trial period (before February 2019) based on five monthly metrics: total sales, number of unique customers, transactions per customer, chips per customer, and average price per unit.

# Composite scoring formula
composite_score = (
    0.5 × pearson_correlation # directional similarity
    + 0.5 × magnitude_distance # 1 − normalised absolute diff
)

# Final trial → control pairings
trial_control = {77: 233, 86: 155, 88: 40}

Trial Store Results

77
Control Store → 233
✓ Significant Uplift
Total sales and number of customers exceeded the 95% confidence threshold in February, March, and April 2019. Strongest performance of the three trial stores.
86
Control Store → 155
✓ Significant Uplift
Consistent uplift across all three trial months on both metrics. Results are statistically significant and closely mirror Store 77's positive outcome.
88
Control Store → 40
— No Significant Uplift
Sales increase was within expected variation bounds. Further investigation by the client is recommended — store-specific factors may explain the divergence from stores 77 and 86.

Statistical Significance Framework

Step Test Purpose Result
1 Welch's t-test (Control pre vs trial) Confirm null hypothesis: no diff within control across periods p > 0.05 ✓
2 Welch's t-test (Trial vs Control pre-trial) Verify trial & control stores are statistically similar pre-trial p > 0.05 ✓
3 T-score vs 95% CI threshold Test if trial-month % diff exceeds pre-trial variation Stores 77 & 86 exceed threshold
